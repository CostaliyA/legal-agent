## I. Executive Summary

The 2023 Private Fund Adviser Rules are **not operative**. The Fifth Circuit held that the Commission exceeded its statutory authority in adopting the Final Rule and **vacated the Final Rule in its entirety**, effective June 5, 2024. *Nat’l Ass’n of Private Fund Managers v. SEC*, No. 23-60471, at 18 n.10, 25 (5th Cir. June 5, 2024) (private-fund-adviser-fifth-circuit.pdf pp. 18 n.10, 25). The court rejected both statutory foundations on which the SEC relied—Advisers Act § 211(h) and § 206(4). *Id.* at 22–25.

The SEC’s November 2024 technical amendments are an executed instrument reflecting that vacatur. They remove the new private-fund rules from the CFR and rescind the related books-and-records and compliance-rule amendments. Technical Amendments, 89 Fed. Reg. 91252, 91252–53 (Nov. 19, 2024) (private-fund-adviser-technical-rescission-2024.pdf pp. 1–2). The technical amendments are effective November 19, 2024, but the Federal Register notice expressly states that the court’s vacatur was effective June 5, 2024. *Id.* at 91252.

Accordingly, every tracker entry that treats the Final Rule’s substantive private-fund requirements as current law should be marked **“Vacated / removed — not operative.”** The SEC’s legal-authority discussion in the Final Rule is agency advocacy, not controlling law; the Fifth Circuit opinion and the SEC’s technical rescission control. The vacatur does **not**, however, eliminate separate fiduciary, antifraud, contractual, or other regulatory duties. Those require separate analysis.

## III. Rule-by-Rule Status

The Fifth Circuit’s holding was categorical: because promulgation of the Final Rule was unauthorized, “no part of it can stand,” and the court “VACATE[d] the Final Rule.” Fifth Cir. op. at 25 (p. 25). The court also expressly rejected the SEC’s reliance on §§ 203(d) and 211(a), and held that the § 204 records-maintenance amendment fell with the challenged rules because it was “obligated by the challenged rules.” *Id.* at 18 n.10.

### A. Quarterly Statement Rule — **Vacated / Removed**

- **Final Rule vehicle:** Rule 211(h)(1)-1.
- **Status:** Not operative. Vacated June 5, 2024; removed from CFR by technical amendments effective Nov. 19, 2024. Fifth Cir. op. at 25; 89 Fed. Reg. at 91252–53 (tech. amend. pp. 1–2).
- **Authority/timing:** SEC relied on §§ 206(4) and 211(h). The Fifth Circuit held § 211(h) applies to “retail customers,” not private-fund investors, and that the SEC exceeded its authority in relying on that section. Fifth Cir. op. at 22. The court also held § 206(4) could not support the Final Rule because the anti-fraud rationale was pretextual and lacked the required definitional and close-nexus showing. *Id.* at 22–25.
- **Tracker instruction:** Mark as vacated/removed. Do not maintain as a current SEC reporting requirement. Contractual reporting obligations in fund documents remain separate.

### B. Mandatory Private Fund Adviser Audit Rule — **Vacated / Removed**

- **Final Rule vehicle:** Rule 206(4)-10; Rule 206(4)-9 was reserved.
- **Status:** Not operative. Vacated June 5, 2024; removed by technical amendments. Fifth Cir. op. at 25; 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2) (removing §§ 275.206(4)-9 and 275.206(4)-10).
- **Authority/timing:** Same controlling holdings: § 211(h) is retail-customer focused; § 206(4) cannot support the rule as adopted. Fifth Cir. op. at 22–25.
- **Separate point:** The Final Rule’s audit requirement would have effectively displaced the custody rule’s surprise-examination option for private-fund advisers. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63245–46 (FR p. 46). Because the audit rule was vacated, that displacement is not operative. The existing custody rule and its surprise-examination option require separate analysis.

### C. Adviser-Led Secondaries Rule — **Vacated / Removed**

- **Final Rule vehicle:** Rule 211(h)(1)-2.
- **Status:** Not operative. Vacated June 5, 2024; removed by technical amendments. Fifth Cir. op. at 25; 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2).
- **Authority/timing:** Same. The Fifth Circuit rejected the SEC’s §§ 206(4) and 211(h) authority for the Final Rule as a whole. Fifth Cir. op. at 22–25.
- **Tracker instruction:** Remove the fairness-opinion/valuation-opinion and relationship-disclosure requirements as current rule requirements. Any contractual or fiduciary disclosure/consent obligations remain separate.

### D. Restricted Activities Rule — **Vacated / Removed**

- **Final Rule vehicle:** Rule 211(h)(2)-1.
- **Status:** Not operative. Vacated June 5, 2024; removed by technical amendments. Fifth Cir. op. at 25; 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2).
- **Scope as adopted:** The Final Rule used disclosure-based and certain consent-based exceptions for specified activities, including investigation expenses, regulatory/compliance/examination expenses, clawback tax reductions, certain non-pro-rata allocations, and borrowing from a private fund client. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63211–12 (FR pp. 6–7). Those rule-based exceptions and prohibitions are no longer operative.
- **Residual point:** The SEC stated that it was not adopting proposed prohibitions on fees for unperformed services and indemnification because that activity is “generally already” contrary to the federal fiduciary duty and antifraud provisions. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63212 (FR p. 7). That statement is not an operative rule, but it flags separate fiduciary/antifraud risk.

### E. Preferential Treatment Rule — **Vacated / Removed**

- **Final Rule vehicle:** The preferential-treatment rule in the 211(h)(2) series. The technical amendments remove §§ 275.211(h)(2)-1 through 275.211(h)(2)-3 en bloc. 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2).
- **Status:** Not operative. Vacated June 5, 2024; removed by technical amendments. Fifth Cir. op. at 25; 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253.
- **Substance as adopted:** The Final Rule prohibited certain preferential redemption rights and preferential portfolio-holdings/exposure information where the adviser reasonably expected a material, negative effect on other investors, and required disclosure of other preferential treatment, subject to exceptions. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63212 (FR p. 7).
- **Tracker instruction:** Mark vacated/removed. Side-letter and conflicts disclosure obligations under fiduciary duty, antifraud provisions, and contracts require separate analysis.

### F. Compliance Rule Amendment — Written Documentation of Annual Review — **Written-Documentation Requirement Vacated; Annual Review Remains**

- **Final Rule vehicle:** Amendment to Rule 206(4)-7(b).
- **Status:** The Final Rule amendment would have required advisers to “review and document in writing” the annual compliance review. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63289 (FR p. 84); 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2). The technical amendments revise Rule 206(4)-7(b) back to require review, but **not** written documentation:

  > “(b) Annual review. Review, no less frequently than annually, the adequacy of the policies and procedures established pursuant to this section and the effectiveness of their implementation[.]”

  89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2).
- **Tracker instruction:** Mark the written-documentation requirement as vacated/removed. Retain a separate tracker item for the surviving annual-review obligation under Rule 206(4)-7(b).

### G. Books-and-Records Amendments — **Vacated / Removed**

- **Final Rule vehicle:** Amendments to Rule 204-2, including added paragraphs (a)(20) through (24) and (a)(7)(v).
- **Status:** The Fifth Circuit held the § 204 records amendment was “obligated by the challenged rules” and therefore vacated with them. Fifth Cir. op. at 18 n.10. The technical amendments remove paragraph (a)(7)(v) and remove and reserve paragraphs (a)(20) through (24). 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2).
- **Tracker instruction:** Remove private-fund-specific recordkeeping entries tied to the vacated rules. The baseline Rule 204-2 recordkeeping obligations otherwise remain for separate analysis.

### H. Proposed “Certain Adviser Misconduct” Provisions — **Not Adopted in the Final Rule**

- **Status:** The proposed prohibitions on fees for unperformed services and on indemnification/limiting liability were **not adopted** in the Final Rule. The SEC explained that the activity “generally already runs contrary to an adviser’s obligations to its clients under the Federal fiduciary duty,” and that much of the indemnification activity is already prohibited by the fiduciary duty and antifraud provisions. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63212 (FR p. 7).
- **Tracker instruction:** Do not mark these as vacated final rules. Mark as “proposed but not adopted.” They remain relevant only as potential fiduciary/antifraud enforcement risk.

## IV. Why the Authority Failed

The Fifth Circuit’s statutory analysis is the key conflict-resolving authority. The SEC’s Final Rule defended its authority under §§ 206(4) and 211(h). 88 Fed. Reg. at 63213 & n.64–65 (FR p. 8). The court rejected that defense.

1. **Section 211(h) is limited to retail customers.**  
   The court held that § 211(h) applies to “retail customers,” not private-fund investors, and that the SEC therefore exceeded its statutory authority in relying on that section to adopt the Final Rule. Fifth Cir. op. at 22. The court emphasized the Dodd-Frank Act’s structure: Title IV specifically addressed private-fund advisers, while the SEC relied on Title IX’s “retail customer” provisions. *Id.* at 10, 20–22.

2. **Section 206(4) cannot support the Final Rule.**  
   The court held that the Final Rule’s “anti-fraud” measure was pretextual; the SEC failed to define the fraudulent or deceptive acts the rule was meant to prevent; the rule lacked a “close nexus” to fraud; and a failure to disclose cannot be deceptive without a duty to disclose. *Id.* at 23–24. The court further held that the adviser’s duty runs to its client—the fund—not the fund’s investors, and that the SEC cannot use § 206(4) to regulate private funds’ internal governance structure. *Id.* at 24.

3. **The remedy was total vacatur.**  
   Under APA § 706, the court “shall” set aside unlawful agency action; because promulgation was unauthorized, “no part of it can stand.” *Id.* at 25. The court also rejected §§ 203(d) and 211(a) as authority and held the records amendment fell with the rules. *Id.* at 18 n.10.

4. **The 2024 technical amendments implement, rather than change, that result.**  
   The SEC removed the rule text and rescinded the related amendments “to reflect the court’s vacatur.” 89 Fed. Reg. at 91252 (tech. amend. p. 1). The notice states the court’s vacatur was effective June 5, 2024. *Id.*

## V. Residual Adviser Duties Requiring Separate Analysis

Vacatur of the Final Rule does not mean “no obligations.” The following require separate review, and the tracker should not conflate them with the vacated rules.

### 1. Federal Fiduciary Duty and Antifraud Provisions

The Advisers Act fiduciary duty remains enforceable through the antifraud provisions. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63213 n.64 (FR p. 8). The SEC’s Final Rule also stated that the rule-based disclosure/consent exceptions did “not change the applicability of any other disclosure and consent obligations, whether under law, rule, regulation, contract, or otherwise,” and described the adviser’s fiduciary obligation to make full and fair disclosure of conflicts and material facts. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63212 n.58 (FR p. 7).

The Fifth Circuit’s statement that the duty “extends to the client alone, which is the fund, not the investors in the fund,” Fifth Cir. op. at 24, must be analyzed carefully. It does not eliminate antifraud liability; it limits the § 206(4) rationale the SEC used. Whether particular investors are “clients,” whether the fund is the sole client, and what disclosure/consent is required are fact-specific and unresolved by the excerpts.

### 2. Contractual Fund Documents, Side Letters, and LPAC Arrangements

Private funds are “free to negotiate fund agreements” concerning periodic financial reports, investor input on fees, and redemption terms. Fifth Cir. op. at 20. Those contractual provisions survive the rule vacatur. The tracker should separately flag:

- Fund governing documents;
- Side letters and preferential terms;
- LPAC or board consent provisions;
- Reporting, audit, redemption, and fee/expense covenants.

These are contract questions, not Rule 211(h) questions.

### 3. Compliance Rule Annual Review

Rule 206(4)-7(b) still requires an annual review of the adequacy of compliance policies and procedures and the effectiveness of their implementation. 89 Fed. Reg. at 91253 (tech. amend. p. 2). The written-documentation requirement added by the vacated Final Rule is gone. Advisers should maintain a separate analysis of the surviving annual-review obligation.

### 4. Custody Rule and Surprise Examination

The vacated audit rule would have eliminated the custody rule’s surprise-examination option for private-fund advisers. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63245–46 (FR p. 46). Because the audit rule is vacated, that displacement is not operative. The existing custody rule and the availability/terms of surprise examinations require separate analysis.

### 5. Marketing Rule, Private Placement Rules, and Antifraud Provisions

The SEC’s release stated that information and disclosure about private-fund investments, performance, fees, and expenses remain subject to the antifraud provisions, the marketing rule, and private-placement rules, notwithstanding negotiated disclosure. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63210 (FR p. 5). Those separate regimes are outside the vacated Final Rule.

### 6. Fees, Expenses, and Conflicts

The SEC’s statements that fees for unperformed services and indemnification are “generally already” contrary to fiduciary duty and antifraud provisions, 88 Fed. Reg. at 63212 (FR p. 7), are not operative rules. They are, however, relevant to separate fiduciary/antifraud risk analysis. The same is true for preferential redemption rights and side-letter conflicts: the rule is vacated, but the underlying conflict-disclosure and consent obligations require separate review.

### 7. Adviser-Led Secondaries

The fairness-opinion/valuation-opinion requirement is vacated. But conflict disclosure, consent, valuation, and fiduciary-duty issues in adviser-led secondaries remain for separate analysis. The SEC’s release discussion of third-party valuation checks, 88 Fed. Reg. at 63252 (FR p. 52), is not an operative rule after vacatur.

## VI. Unresolved Matters and Caveats

1. **The actual tracker was not supplied.** The status mapping above is based on the rule categories in the Final Rule and technical amendments. If the tracker uses different labels or sub-rule numbers, reconcile them to the rule titles and the technical amendment’s en bloc removal of §§ 275.211(h)(1)-1 through 275.211(h)(2)-3.

2. **Appeal/rehearing status is not shown in the excerpts.** The technical amendments treat the Fifth Circuit vacatur as effective June 5, 2024. Whether further appellate proceedings occurred is not established by the supplied sources.

3. **Pre-vacatur compliance period.** The Final Rule had a stated effective date of November 13, 2023, and compliance dates in Section IV. 88 Fed. Reg. at 63206 (FR p. 1). The excerpts do not supply the full compliance schedule or whether any client took steps in reliance before June 5, 2024. That requires a facts-and-reliance review.

4. **Contractual incorporation.** Whether any fund documents, side letters, or service agreements incorporate the vacated rules by reference is unresolved and requires document review.

5. **Client status and fiduciary scope.** The Fifth Circuit’s statement that the duty runs to the fund, not investors, Fifth Cir. op. at 24, requires fact-specific analysis of the advisory relationship, investor status, and any separate duties.

6. **Replacement rulemaking.** The excerpts do not show any SEC replacement rulemaking. Do not assume one exists.

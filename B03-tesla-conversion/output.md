## Substantive Contents for `rights-comparison.docx`

**Title:** Rights Comparison — Tesla, Inc. Proposed Texas Redomestication vs. Current Delaware Instruments (as of April 29, 2024)

### I. Scope, Sources, and Status

This memorandum compares the current Delaware governing instruments with the proposed Texas governing instruments described in Tesla’s 2024 definitive proxy statement. It is based only on the supplied excerpts from `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`; the complete annexes and any post-April 29, 2024 vote or filing are not included in the excerpts.

The key status distinction is:

- **Current, effective instruments:** Tesla’s Delaware Charter and Delaware Bylaws, included as Annex F and Annex G to the proxy. The proxy states that, following completion of the Texas Redomestication, the Delaware Charter and Delaware Bylaws “will no longer be in effect.” `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 40.
- **Proposed, conditional instruments:** The Texas Charter and Texas Bylaws. The proxy states that approval of Proposal Three “will constitute approval of the Texas Charter and Texas Bylaws.” They are not described as effective as of the proxy date. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 40.
- **Conversion mechanics:** The proposal is to convert the Delaware corporation into a Texas corporation under DGCL § 266 and TBOC Title 1, Chapter 10, Subchapter C, pursuant to a Plan of Conversion included as Annex A. Approval of Proposal Three would constitute approval of the Plan of Conversion. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 31.
- **Timing and abandonment:** If stockholders approve, the conversion is anticipated to become effective “as soon as practicable following the 2024 Annual Meeting,” after filings with the Texas and Delaware Secretaries of State. The Board may delay or terminate and abandon the Plan of Conversion before the effective time, even after stockholder approval, if it determines doing so is in the best interests of the Company and stockholders. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 32.
- **No final effectiveness shown:** The excerpts do not show the stockholder vote result, the actual filings, or the effective time. Accordingly, this memorandum treats the Texas Charter and Texas Bylaws as proposals, not as operative terms.

The proxy also describes the *Tornetta* Opinion as the Delaware Court of Chancery’s post-trial opinion finding breach of fiduciary duties and ordering rescission of Mr. Musk’s 2018 CEO Performance Award. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 32. That court holding is distinct from the redomestication proposal. Mr. Musk’s X poll and posts about moving to Texas are described as background; they are not the Board or stockholder approval mechanism. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 28, 32.

### II. Approval Mechanics

#### A. Board and Special Committee Process

- The Special Committee had one member, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson. It was formed on February 10, 2024. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 28, 30.
- Before its final approval meeting on April 16, 2024, the Special Committee met 16 times for more than 26 hours and spent more than 200 hours outside meetings on the matter. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 30.
- On March 5, 2024, the Board expanded the Special Committee’s authority to decide whether Mr. Musk’s 2018 CEO Performance Award should be ratified at the same time as any redomestication vote and to address related disclosures. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 29.
- On April 16, 2024, the Special Committee recommended the Texas Redomestication and related matters. Its report is dated April 12, 2024 and is attached as Annex E. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 35.
- The Board met on April 9, April 13, and April 16, 2024, with Mr. Musk and Kimbal Musk recusing themselves. On April 16, 2024, the Board determined that the Texas Redomestication was in the best interests of the Company and stockholders, approved it, directed submission to stockholders, and recommended approval. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 36.
- The Board adopted the Texas Redomestication Resolution, attached as Annex D, and submitted it to stockholders under DGCL § 266. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 35–36.

#### B. Stockholder Vote Requirement for Proposal Three

The disclosed vote requirement for Proposal Three is dual:

1. **Conversion Standard:** a majority of the outstanding shares of Tesla stock entitled to vote thereon; and  
2. **Conversion Disinterested Standard:** a majority of the voting power of Tesla shares not owned, directly or indirectly, by Mr. Musk or Kimbal Musk, present in person or represented by proxy and entitled to vote thereon.

`tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 171.

The proxy states that abstentions are counted only toward tabulations of voting power present and entitled for the Conversion Disinterested Standard and have the same effect as votes against the proposal for both standards. Broker non-votes have the same effect as votes against for the Conversion Standard and no effect on the Conversion Disinterested Standard. Brokers do not have discretionary authority to vote on the proposal. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 73.

The Special Committee had recommended that the redomestication be conditioned on approval by a majority of votes cast by non-Musk-affiliated stockholders. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 35. The excerpts do not reconcile that recommendation with the operative Q&A formulation at p. 171. For this comparison, the p. 171 disclosed proposal requirement is treated as the operative stockholder vote condition.

#### C. Effect of Approval; Proposal Four Is Separate

Approval of Proposal Three would approve the Plan of Conversion and, per the proxy, the Texas Charter and Texas Bylaws. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 31, 40. It would not itself complete the conversion; filings and the effective time remain conditions. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 32.

Proposal Four—ratification of the 2018 CEO Performance Award—is a separate stockholder proposal with its own vote requirement. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 171. It is not a change to the charter or bylaws, although the Special Committee considered its timing and related disclosures in connection with the redomestication process. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 29.

### III. Director and Board Rights

#### 1. Board Vacancies

- **Current Delaware Charter/Bylaws:** Vacancies may be filled only by a majority of the remaining directors or by a sole remaining director, and not by stockholders. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 41, 43.
- **Proposed Texas Charter:** The TBOC permits vacancies to be filled by a majority of remaining directors, a sole remaining director, or holders of a majority of outstanding shares. It also prevents the board from filling more than two vacancies caused by an increase in board size between annual meetings, and directors appointed or elected to fill a vacancy may serve only until the next annual meeting. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 41.
- **Proposed Texas Bylaws:** Vacancies may be filled in any manner permitted by the TBOC, subject to the TBOC’s limits. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 43.

**Change:** The proposed Texas documents would add a stockholder vacancy-filling right and TBOC interim-term limits. This is a director/shareholder power shift, not merely a recodification.

#### 2. Special Shareholder Meetings and Cancellation

- **Current Delaware Charter:** Special stockholder meetings may be called only by the Board, chairperson, CEO, or president (in the absence of a CEO), and may not be called by stockholders. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 42.
- **Proposed Texas Charter:** Special meetings may be called by the Board, chairperson, CEO, president, or shareholders holding 50% of shares entitled to vote on the proposed action. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 42.
- **Cancellation:** The Delaware Bylaws allow the Board to cancel, postpone, or reschedule a special stockholder meeting. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 44. The proposed Texas Charter does not give the Board the right to cancel a special meeting, although it retains postponement/rescheduling; the proposed Texas Bylaws allow the Board to cancel a special meeting not called by shareholders. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 42, 44.

**Change:** Texas would expand shareholders’ ability to call special meetings at the 50% threshold and restrict Board cancellation of shareholder-called meetings.

#### 3. Action by Written Consent

- **Current Delaware Bylaws:** Stockholder action by written consent is prohibited. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 43.
- **Proposed Texas Bylaws:** Because the TBOC requires shareholders to have the option, the proposed Texas Bylaws set the standard at unanimous written consent—the highest standard permitted. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 43.

**Change:** A formal written-consent right would exist in Texas, but only with unanimous consent, which the proxy describes as unlikely given Tesla’s widely held shareholder base. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 42–43.

#### 4. Board Committees

- **Current Delaware Bylaws:** No committee may approve or adopt, or recommend to stockholders, any action expressly required by the DGCL to be submitted to stockholders, except election or removal of directors; nor may a committee adopt, amend, or repeal bylaws. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 44.
- **Proposed Texas Bylaws:** Committees may not approve or adopt, or recommend to shareholders, any action expressly required by the TBOC to be submitted to shareholders or otherwise non-delegable, and may not adopt, amend, or repeal bylaws. The TBOC also lists specific prohibited committee actions, including amending the certificate of formation, approving mergers/share exchanges/conversions, recommending sales of substantially all assets, recommending winding up, filling board vacancies, electing or removing officers, setting committee compensation, and altering certain board resolutions. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 44–45.

**Change:** The proposed Texas Bylaws would incorporate the TBOC’s more detailed committee prohibitions. The practical effect depends on the final Texas Bylaws and TBOC provisions, which are summarized but not reproduced in the excerpts.

#### 5. Indemnification

- **Current Delaware Bylaws:** Authorize indemnification of directors and officers to the fullest extent permitted by Delaware law. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 42.
- **Proposed Texas Charter:** Authorizes indemnification to the fullest extent permitted by the TBOC. The proxy notes TBOC limits where the person is liable to the corporation or received an improper personal benefit—indemnification is then limited to reasonable expenses actually incurred—and no indemnification is available for willful or intentional misconduct, breach of the duty of loyalty, or bad-faith breach of duty. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 42–43.

**Change:** The source and statutory limits of indemnification would shift from Delaware law to the TBOC. The excerpt does not show the full advancement provision or any exculpation provision, so the net practical effect cannot be fully determined from the excerpts.

#### 6. Director Consideration of Constituencies

- **Delaware:** Except for public benefit corporations, Delaware directors have no express statutory authority to consider other constituencies; fiduciary duties generally require maximizing long-term stockholder value. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 38–39, 62.
- **Proposed Texas law:** The TBOC expressly permits directors to consider long-term and short-term interests of the corporation and shareholders, including continued independence, and permits consideration of social, charitable, or environmental purposes. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 38–39, 62.

**Change:** Texas law would give directors express statutory space to consider non-stockholder constituencies. Tesla states it does not expect this to change how it operates but sees symbolic value in aligning with Texas law. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 38.

#### 7. Removal of Directors

The proxy’s state-law comparison notes that Delaware and Texas both regulate removal of directors, including special rules for classified boards and cumulative voting. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 49. The Texas column in the supplied excerpt is truncated, and the proposed Texas Charter’s removal provision is not shown. The exact change to Tesla’s director-removal rights is therefore unresolved on the supplied excerpts.

### IV. Shareholder Rights and Voting

#### 1. Fundamental Transactions Voting Threshold

- **Current Delaware Charter:** No higher-than-default voting threshold; mergers, conversions, sales of substantially all assets, and similar matters generally require a majority of outstanding shares entitled to vote. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 41.
- **TBOC default:** Fundamental business transactions generally require two-thirds of the outstanding shares of each class unless the charter specifies a lower threshold, not less than a majority. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 41, 53.
- **Proposed Texas Charter:** Contains language setting the default voting thresholds at a majority standard unless a different standard is specified elsewhere. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 41.

**Change:** On the disclosed proposed terms, the Texas Charter would preserve a majority voting standard rather than accept the TBOC’s two-thirds default. If the Texas Charter did not contain that language, shareholder approval thresholds would increase.

#### 2. Charter Amendments and Authorized Shares

- **Delaware:** Subject to exceptions, charter amendments require board approval and majority stockholder approval. Delaware permits certain board action without stockholder approval for forward stock splits in limited circumstances. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 47.
- **Texas:** Once stock has been issued, the TBOC does not permit the board unilaterally to increase or decrease authorized capital stock without shareholder approval, and there is no express forward-stock-split exception. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 47.

**Potential change:** The TBOC would reduce board flexibility to increase authorized shares without stockholder approval. The excerpts do not show whether the proposed Texas Charter contains any provision addressing this; the point remains subject to the full Texas Charter.

#### 3. Bylaw Amendments

Under Delaware law, stockholders entitled to vote have the right to amend, repeal, or adopt bylaws, and the board may also have that right if the charter so provides. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 57. Under the TBOC, the board generally may amend, repeal, or adopt bylaws, but shareholders may also do so, and the certificate of formation may reserve bylaw power exclusively to shareholders. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 57. The proposed Texas Bylaws’ exact allocation is not reproduced in the excerpts.

#### 4. Proxies

- **Current Delaware Bylaws:** A proxy is valid for three years from execution unless it provides for a longer period. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 44.
- **Proposed Texas Bylaws:** No proxy may be voted or acted upon after eleven months from its date unless the proxy provides for a longer period, reflecting the TBOC default. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 44.

**Change:** The default proxy duration would shorten from three years to eleven months.

#### 5. Anti-Takeover and Business Combination Provisions

The proxy identifies anti-takeover protections as the “most potentially important area” of difference. Both states permit poison pills and business-combination provisions, but the ownership thresholds differ: 20% in Texas and 15% in Delaware. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 40. The TBOC also expressly permits directors to consider long-term shareholder benefit in taking action. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 66.

**Potential change:** The Texas business-combination threshold would be higher than Delaware’s, which may affect takeover defenses and related shareholder/economic rights. The full Texas Charter and Bylaws are not supplied, so whether Tesla adopts additional defenses is unresolved.

#### 6. Notice, Adjournment, and Quorum

The proxy’s state-law comparison identifies differences in adjournment notice and quorum rules. For example, Delaware has specific notice rules if a meeting is adjourned for more than 30 days or a new record date is fixed; the TBOC lacks a specific provision on notice for adjourned meetings. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 50. The excerpt at p. 52 also shows differences in quorum and voting rules, but the text is truncated. The exact effect on Tesla’s proposed Texas Bylaws is unresolved.

### V. Litigation Rights

#### 1. Forum Selection

- **Delaware:** A Delaware corporation’s charter or bylaws may require internal corporate claims to be brought in Delaware courts, and may not prohibit bringing such claims in Delaware. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 66.
- **Texas:** A Texas entity’s governing documents may require internal entity claims, including derivative claims, to be brought only in a Texas court. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 66.

**Unresolved:** The excerpts do not show whether the proposed Texas Charter or Texas Bylaws actually contain a Texas forum-selection clause. The proxy only describes what Texas law permits.

#### 2. Derivative Suits and Demand

- **Delaware:** A stockholder must make a demand on the board or show demand futility. Demand is futile if at least half the board received a material personal benefit, faces a substantial likelihood of liability, or lacks independence from someone who did. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 66–67.
- **Texas:** Texas is described as a universal-demand jurisdiction. A shareholder may not institute a derivative proceeding until the 91st day after a written demand is filed, unless the demand is rejected, the corporation is suffering irreparable injury, or waiting would cause irreparable injury. The shareholder must also fairly and adequately represent the corporation’s interests. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 67.

**Change:** Texas would eliminate the Delaware demand-futility path and impose a universal written-demand waiting period, subject to exceptions.

#### 3. Jury Trials

Delaware Court of Chancery proceedings generally do not provide jury trials. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 67. Under Texas law, civil cases generally carry a right to a jury trial if timely demanded and the jury fee is paid. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 67.

**Change:** Texas litigation would generally make jury trials available in civil cases, while Delaware Court of Chancery litigation does not.

#### 4. Appraisal and Dissenters’ Rights

The proxy compares Delaware appraisal rights and Texas dissenters’ rights. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 67–69. The excerpts are truncated and do not provide the complete Texas appraisal provision or the full proposed Texas Charter treatment. The precise change to Tesla stockholders’ appraisal rights is therefore unresolved from the supplied excerpts.

#### 5. Indemnification and Advancement

As noted above, the Delaware Bylaws authorize indemnification to the fullest extent permitted by Delaware law, while the proposed Texas Charter authorizes indemnification to the fullest extent permitted by the TBOC, with TBOC-specific limitations. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 42–43. The proxy also compares advancement of expenses under Delaware and Texas law. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 64. The full advancement provisions of the proposed Texas Bylaws are not supplied.

#### 6. Business Courts

The proxy notes that Texas created a specialized business court system set to open on September 1, 2024, while Delaware has an established business court with a large body of corporate case law. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 35, 39. This is a litigation-forum consideration, not a stockholder right in the governing documents.

### VI. Economic Rights

#### 1. Dividends and Buybacks

The Special Committee evaluated and confirmed that the Texas Redomestication would not materially alter other economic rights, including the Company’s ability to pay dividends or buy back stock. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 34.

#### 2. Franchise Tax and Filing Fees

Tesla currently pays approximately $250,000 per year in Delaware franchise taxes. The proxy states that this will no longer be required if the Texas Redomestication is completed, producing a net annual savings of approximately $250,000. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 34, 69. Texas tax obligations would not change except potentially with respect to calculation of gross income for future debt repurchases at a discount. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 69.

#### 3. Accounting, Tax, and Trading Effects

The proxy states that the redomestication will not have materially adverse accounting, tax, or other financial implications and will not affect public trading of Tesla shares. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 34. For U.S. federal income tax purposes, Tesla believes no gain or loss will be recognized by the Company, the Texas corporation, or stockholders receiving Texas Corporation common stock for Delaware Corporation common stock; tax basis and holding period generally carry over. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, pp. 70–71. Tesla has not requested or received a tax opinion or IRS ruling, and the proxy does not address state, local, or foreign tax consequences. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 71.

#### 4. Conversion of Shares, Options, and Awards

Outstanding options to purchase Delaware Corporation common stock will convert into options to purchase an equal number of Texas Corporation common stock on the same terms and conditions. Other stock awards will convert into equivalent awards with the same terms. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 70. The proxy states that the conversion will not affect obligations or liabilities incurred before conversion, personal liability incurred before conversion, or choice of law for matters arising before conversion. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 70.

#### 5. No Change in Business, Jobs, Management, or Location

The proxy states that the redomestication will not change Tesla’s business, jobs, management, properties, office or facility locations, number of employees, obligations, assets, liabilities, or net worth, other than costs related to the redomestication. Management, including all directors and officers, will remain the same and hold identical positions with the Texas corporation. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 70.

#### 6. Partly Paid Stock

- **Current Delaware Bylaws:** Permit the corporation to issue partly paid stock. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 45.
- **Proposed Texas Bylaws:** Do not provide for partly paid stock because the TBOC requires full consideration for shares before issuance. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 45.

**Change:** The proposed Texas Bylaws would eliminate the partly paid stock authorization.

#### 7. Authorized Share Increases

As noted above, the TBOC would require shareholder approval for board-initiated increases or decreases in authorized capital stock after issuance, without an express forward-stock-split exception. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 47. This is an economic-governance change that could affect future capital-raising and stock-split mechanics.

#### 8. Anti-Takeover Thresholds

The Texas business-combination threshold would be 20% rather than Delaware’s 15%. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 40. This may affect the rights of large shareholders and the board’s defensive flexibility, although the full practical effect depends on the final Texas Charter and Bylaws.

### VIII. Unresolved Matters and Limitations

1. **No evidence of effectiveness.** The excerpts do not show the 2024 Annual Meeting vote results, the Certificate of Conversion filings, or the effective time. The Texas Charter and Texas Bylaws therefore remain proposals for purposes of this comparison.

2. **Full annexes not supplied.** The complete Texas Charter and Texas Bylaws, the Delaware Charter and Bylaws, the Plan of Conversion, the Texas Redomestication Resolution, and the Special Committee Report are not reproduced in the excerpts. Provisions not summarized in the proxy—such as exclusive forum, exculpation, preferred stock terms, quorum, director removal, advancement, and any supermajority provisions—cannot be finally compared.

3. **Vote-formulation discrepancy.** The Special Committee recommended a majority of votes cast by non-Musk-affiliated stockholders, while the proxy Q&A discloses a majority of the voting power of non-Musk shares present and entitled to vote. The excerpts do not reconcile these formulations. This memorandum uses the Q&A formulation at p. 171 as the disclosed operative proposal requirement.

4. **Director removal.** The state-law comparison at p. 49 is truncated, and the proposed Texas Charter’s removal provision is not shown. The exact change to removal rights is unresolved.

5. **Forum-selection clause.** The proxy states what Texas law permits but does not show whether the proposed Texas Charter or Bylaws actually adopt a Texas forum-selection clause.

6. **Appraisal/dissenters’ rights.** The excerpted comparison is incomplete. The precise differences between Delaware appraisal and Texas dissenters’ rights, as applied to Tesla’s proposed documents, are unresolved.

7. **Tax consequences.** The proxy addresses only U.S. federal income tax consequences and states that no tax opinion or IRS ruling was obtained. State, local, and foreign tax consequences are not addressed. `tesla-definitive-proxy-2024.pdf`, p. 71.

8. **Proposal Four and compensation.** Proposal Four is a separate ratification proposal. The excerpts do not establish its outcome or its effect on the charter/bylaw comparison.

# Closing Issues Register — Microsoft / Activision Blizzard

## 1. Scope and source discipline

This register treats the **Agreement and Plan of Merger dated January 18, 2022** as the operative executed deal instrument. The later Microsoft annual-report disclosure is treated as later public disclosure, not as an executed amendment. The FTC complaint, district-court preliminary-injunction opinion, and CMA interim order are litigation/regulatory materials, not deal terms. No supplied excerpt confirms the actual closing, the Effective Time, or final post-closing holdings; the later annual report still says Microsoft is “work[ing] toward closing” subject to regulatory approvals and customary conditions. Pretrial Record p. 216; Microsoft 2023 Annual Report pp. 968, 2533.

## 2. Deal structure

- **Parties and form.** Microsoft Corporation is “Parent”; Anchorage Merger Sub Inc. is a Delaware corporation and wholly owned Microsoft subsidiary (“Merger Sub”); Activision Blizzard, Inc. is a Delaware corporation (“Company”). The merger agreement is dated January 18, 2022. Pretrial Record p. 216.
- **Reverse triangular merger.** At the Closing, Merger Sub will merge with and into the Company; Merger Sub’s separate existence ceases; the Company survives as the surviving corporation and a subsidiary of Parent. Pretrial Record p. 231 (Section 2.1). The Effective Time is tied to filing of a Certificate of Merger with the Delaware Secretary of State. Pretrial Record p. 231 (Section 2.2).
- **Consideration.** “Merger Consideration” is **$95.00 in cash, without interest**. Pretrial Record p. 223 (definition (yy)). At the Effective Time, each outstanding share of Company common stock—other than Owned Company Shares and Dissenting Company Shares—is cancelled and converted into the right to receive the Merger Consideration. Pretrial Record p. 232 (Section 2.7(a)(iii)).
- **Later valuation disclosure.** Microsoft’s 2023 annual report describes the transaction as **$95.00 per share in an all-cash transaction valued at $68.7 billion, inclusive of Activision Blizzard’s net cash**, and states that Activision Blizzard shareholders approved the acquisition. Microsoft 2023 Annual Report pp. 968, 2533. The $68.7 billion figure is a disclosed transaction valuation, not the operative per-share consideration formula.

## 3. Closing conditions

### 3.1 Mutual conditions — Section 7.1

The obligations of Parent, Merger Sub, and the Company are subject to satisfaction or permitted waiver of:

1. **Requisite Stockholder Approval** at the Company Stockholder Meeting. Pretrial Record p. 276 (Section 7.1(a)).
2. **Competition Approvals** — expiration/termination of HSR waiting periods and other listed Laws, or requisite clearances/consents/approvals, **without imposition of a Burdensome Condition**. Pretrial Record p. 276 (Section 7.1(b)).
3. **Other Regulatory Approvals** under the Laws listed in Section 7.1(c) of the Company Disclosure Letter, again without a Burdensome Condition. Pretrial Record p. 276 (Section 7.1(c)).
4. **No Prohibitive Laws or Injunctions** — no temporary restraining order, preliminary or permanent injunction, judgment, order, legal restraint, or prohibition preventing consummation, and no statute/rule/regulation/order that prohibits, makes illegal, enjoins, or imposes/seeks to impose a Burdensome Condition. Pretrial Record p. 276 (Section 7.1(d)).

The definition of **“Burdensome Condition”** is cross-referenced to Section 6.2(b) in the agreement’s term table, but the definition itself is not reproduced in the supplied excerpts. Pretrial Record p. 227. The Company Disclosure Letter lists for Sections 7.1(b) and 7.1(c) are also not supplied.

### 3.2 Parent/Merger Sub conditions — Section 7.2

Parent and Merger Sub’s obligations are separately conditioned on:

- Company representations and warranties being true and correct, subject to the specified materiality, MAE, and de minimis standards. Pretrial Record pp. 276–277 (Section 7.2(a)).
- Company performance in all material respects of its covenants and obligations. Pretrial Record p. 277 (Section 7.2(b)).
- Delivery of a Company officer’s certificate certifying satisfaction of Sections 7.2(a) and 7.2(b). Pretrial Record p. 277 (Section 7.2(c)).
- **No Company Material Adverse Effect** having occurred after the date of the agreement and continuing. Pretrial Record p. 277 (Section 7.2(d)).

The definition of **“Company Material Adverse Effect”** is not reproduced in the excerpts.

### 3.3 Company conditions — Section 7.3

The excerpt shows only the heading and the opening words of Section 7.3(a) concerning Parent’s representations and warranties. Pretrial Record p. 277. The full Section 7.3 conditions are **not supplied** and should be obtained before the checklist is finalized.

### 3.4 Later disclosure and regulatory context

- Microsoft’s later annual report states that Activision Blizzard shareholders approved the acquisition, that Microsoft continues to work toward closing subject to required regulatory approvals and other customary closing conditions, and that Microsoft and Activision Blizzard “jointly agreed to extend the merger agreement through October 18, 2023 to allow for additional time to resolve remaining regulatory concerns.” Microsoft 2023 Annual Report pp. 968, 2533.
- The pretrial record recounts that the CMA issued an interim order, the FTC filed a Section 13(b) action and obtained a TRO, and the district court denied the FTC’s preliminary injunction on July 10, 2023. Pretrial Record pp. 9–10. The opinion found the FTC had shown a “High-Performance Console Market,” assumed other product markets, found the relevant geographic market for consoles was the United States, and found Microsoft would have the ability to foreclose Activision content, but concluded the FTC had not adequately shown incentive to foreclose. Pretrial Record p. 10. That preliminary-injunction ruling is not a final antitrust clearance or a final merits adjudication.

### 3.5 Related interim covenants

The proxy summary describes interim conduct-of-business covenants and no-solicitation obligations pending the merger, including ordinary-course operation and termination of alternative-acquisition discussions. Pretrial Record pp. 181–183. It also describes consultation obligations for specified and transaction litigation. Pretrial Record p. 189. These are covenants, not closing conditions; the confidential disclosure letter exceptions are not supplied.

## 5. Fee exposure

### 5.1 Company Termination Fee

- Amount: **$2,270,100,000**. Pretrial Record p. 280 (Section 8.3(b)).
- Payable in the Section 8.3(b)(i) scenario if the agreement is terminated under Section 8.1(c), 8.1(d), or 8.1(e), and either an Acquisition Transaction is consummated or the Company enters into a definitive agreement for one; payment is due within two Business Days after the earlier of entry into the definitive agreement or consummation. For this clause, references to “15%” in the definition of “Acquisition Transaction” are deemed references to “50%.” Pretrial Record p. 280.
- Payable under Section 8.3(b)(ii) if termination occurs under Section 8.1(f) (Company Board Recommendation Change), due within two Business Days. Pretrial Record p. 280.
- Payable under Section 8.3(b)(iii) if termination occurs under Section 8.1(h) (Superior Proposal), payable concurrently with termination. Pretrial Record p. 280.
- The excerpt begins Section 8.3(b)(i) mid-sentence; any preceding conditions or time period are not fully visible. The “Acquisition Transaction” definition is also not supplied.

### 5.2 Parent Termination Fee

- Payable if the agreement is terminated under **Section 8.1(b) due to an injunction arising from Antitrust Laws**, or under **Section 8.1(c)** where all conditions are satisfied or waived other than closing-date conditions and Sections 7.1(b) or 7.1(d) solely in connection with an Antitrust Law, and the Company is not then in material breach. Pretrial Record p. 280.
- Amount depends on when the termination notice is provided:
  - Before January 18, 2023: **$2,000,000,000**.
  - After January 18, 2023 and before April 18, 2023: **$2,500,000,000**.
  - After April 18, 2023: **$3,000,000,000**.  
  Pretrial Record p. 280.
- The fee is subject to a single-payment limitation: neither the Company Termination Fee nor the Parent Termination Fee is payable more than once. Pretrial Record p. 280. The interest/default mechanics in Section 8.3(e) are only partially reproduced.

## 7. Bottom line for the deal team

The executed structure is a **reverse triangular all-cash merger** at **$95.00 per share**, with Microsoft as Parent, Anchorage Merger Sub Inc. as Merger Sub, and Activision Blizzard as the surviving subsidiary. The principal closing gates are **stockholder approval, antitrust/regulatory approvals without a Burdensome Condition, other listed regulatory approvals, and no prohibitive injunction**. The termination architecture includes an outside date, regulatory-injunction trigger, stockholder-approval failure, breach triggers, board-recommendation-change trigger, and Superior Proposal trigger. Fee exposure on the face of the excerpts is **$2.2701 billion** for the Company Termination Fee and up to **$3.0 billion** for the Parent Termination Fee, subject to timing and breach conditions.

The critical unresolved document is the **written extension/amendment supporting the reported October 18, 2023 termination date**, together with the missing definitions, disclosure-letter lists, full Section 7.3, full Section 8.1, and final closing/regulatory records.
